## English

```
Draw an 6 to 8 pane comic strip of the story

story:
Todd stared intently at the acoustic tiles on the ceiling. He was trying to decide if the water stain above the bookshelf looked more like a map of Florida or a deformed potato. Anything was better than making eye contact with Dr. Aris Thorne.

"I’m telling you, doc, I am totally fine," Todd said, letting his hands fall flat against the armrests of the plush leather chair. "Work is fine. My relationship with Sarah is fine. My digestion is fine. I honestly think last month’s panic attack was just too much cold brew on an empty stomach."

Dr. Thorne, a man whose sharply angled beard and wire-rimmed glasses made him look like a predatory owl, did not look convinced. He steepled his fingers, the soft leather of his own chair creaking in the suffocatingly quiet room. 

"Todd," Dr. Thorne murmured, his voice a low, resonant baritone. "The psyche does not conjure a panic attack over a mere beverage. The psyche is a pressure cooker. When we deny our frustrations, they leak out sideways. Tell me, in the entirety of the last seven days, what was the absolute pinnacle of your emotional distress?"

Todd sighed, running a hand over his face. "You want the truth? The absolute truth?"

"I insist upon it."

"The most frustrated I’ve been all week—by a mile—was on Tuesday night. I was playing Super Metroid on the Switch's retro emulator."

Dr. Thorne un-steepled his fingers. "A video game."

"Yeah. It’s a classic. But it has this one part that makes me want to put my controller through the drywall. You fall down this sheer, vertical tunnel, right? And the only way out is to use this bizarre wall-jump game mechanic." Todd sat up a little, his gamer-rage momentarily overriding his therapy-apathy. "It is infuriatingly precise. You have to do a spin-jump into the wall, wait for your character to touch it, press the D-pad in the exact opposite direction for a fraction of a second, and then hit the jump button. If you're off by literally one frame, you just slide down the wall like a wet noodle. I was stuck in that shaft for two and a half hours, just violently bouncing between the same two walls, making zero progress."

Todd leaned back, shaking his head. "So, yeah. That’s my trauma for the week. Bad hitboxes and retro controls."

Silence hung in the room. The mahogany grandfather clock in the corner ticked methodically. 

Dr. Thorne reached over to his side table, picked up a moleskine notebook, and unscrewed his fountain pen. 

"Todd... do you truly not hear the profound confession you’ve just made?" 

Todd blinked. "Confession?"

"Consider the architecture of your so-called 'retro' frustration," Dr. Thorne began, leaning forward, his eyes burning with sudden, terrifying intensity. "You are navigating the subterranean depths of a hostile, alien planet. A dark, biological labyrinth overseen by the game’s ultimate antagonist. And what is the name of this antagonist, Todd?"

"Uh. Mother Brain," Todd said slowly. 

"Mother Brain," Thorne repeated, letting the words roll off his tongue like a fine, dark wine. "A giant, disembodied intelligence sitting in a glass jar, an all-seeing, all-consuming ego that demands the entire ecosystem revolve around her whims. The ultimate, primeval narcissistic mother figure. She controls the planet, Todd. She consumes your agency. And where has she trapped you?"

"In... in a tunnel in Brinstar—"

"In a sheer, pulsating, inescapable vertical shaft," Dr. Thorne interrupted, his voice rising in volume. "The yonic imagery could not possibly be more explicit! You are entombed within the maternal canal, surrounded by the fleshy, claustrophobic walls of the all-encompassing Mother."

"Look, man, it’s mostly just green pixels—"

Dr. Thorne stood up, beginning to pace the Persian rug. "And how must you escape this suffocating, yonic trap? Through the 'wall-jump.' A violent, rhythmic rejection of the boundaries containing you. You hurl yourself against the walls, but you cannot cling to them. To cling is to succumb to the engulfing mother. You must push away! You must perfectly time your separation, bouncing from boundary to boundary, struggling desperately upward toward individuation."

Todd sat frozen, his mouth slightly open. 

"But your timing fails, doesn't it, Todd?" Dr. Thorne whirled around, pointing the fountain pen at him like an accusing finger. "You hesitate. You press against the wall for a fraction of a second too long, secretly yearning for the comfort of the womb, and so you slide back down into the abyss! You lose all momentum. Because deep down, your repressed selfhood is terrified of ascending into the light. You wrap your protagonist in heavy, metallic power-armor to protect your fragile inner child from a hostile world, yet you remain trapped in the birth canal of your own repressed autonomy, fighting a 16-bit manifestation of the mother who never let you become a man!"

Dr. Thorne stood panting slightly, the glow of psychoanalytic revelation radiating from his bespectacled face. He slowly lowered his pen and sat back down, smoothing his tweed trousers. 

"It is quite textbook, really," Thorne concluded softly. "We have much work to do."

Todd stared at the psychoanalyst. He thought about the D-pad on his Switch Pro Controller. He thought about the three little alien monkeys in the game that teach you how to do the jump. 

"Doc," Todd whispered. "I really just think my thumb was sweaty."
```
